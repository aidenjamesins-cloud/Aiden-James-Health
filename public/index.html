Straight answers.

The questions I hear most, answered plainly. Don’t see yours? Call or text (813) 451-0821.

What does it cost to work with you?

Nothing. Carriers pay me a commission when you enroll, so the quote, the comparison, enrollment help and ongoing support are free. Your premium is the same either way.

Can I only sign up during Open Enrollment?

No. Private, medically underwritten plans accept applications any time of year, with no life event needed, and approval can come within days. For Marketplace plans, 2027 Open Enrollment runs November 1 to January 15, and a life event like losing job coverage, moving or having a baby opens a 60-day window.

Is private insurance expensive?

Often it costs less. Private plans are medically underwritten, so healthy people can pay less than they would for a Marketplace plan without tax credits. Your price depends on your age, state, household and health history, which is why I quote it personally instead of guessing.

Are these year-long contracts?

Usually not. Many private plans bill month to month with no long-term contract, so you can change or cancel as your needs change. I’ll confirm the terms of any plan before you apply.

Will a PPO cover me anywhere I go?

Many PPO plans use national networks, so you can see doctors across the country without referrals. Networks vary by plan, so I check your doctors and hospitals before you apply.

What if I have a pre-existing condition?

Tell me up front. Marketplace plans always cover pre-existing conditions. Private plans look at your health history: many conditions can still be covered, but some may be excluded or lead to a decline. I’ll be upfront about what’s realistic, and if private isn’t the right fit, I’ll tell you.

Is a private PPO the same as major medical insurance?

Not always. Some private plans are comprehensive major medical. Others are limited-benefit plans that pay set amounts toward care. I’ll tell you exactly which one you’re looking at.

Why get private insurance if my employer offers benefits?

Sometimes you shouldn’t. Employer plans can be a great deal when your employer pays a big share. But family coverage through work is often expensive, and a private plan stays with you if you change jobs. I’ll compare both side by side.

I’m leaving my job. Is COBRA my only option?

No. COBRA keeps your current plan, but you usually pay the full premium plus up to 2%. Losing job coverage also opens a 60-day Marketplace window, and private plans accept applications year-round. I’ll compare all three before your deadline.

What if I’m already well covered?

I’ll tell you, and that’s it. No follow-up sales calls.

What happens after I enroll?

I stay your point of contact. Claims, billing and renewal questions come straight to me.

Can you help my business offer coverage?

Yes. I help small businesses compare and set up group health plans, and I can show you individual options for your team too.
